### How Loud Is Loud Enough? (decibels explained)
Decibels (dB) measure loudness, and every 10 dB sounds roughly twice as loud to most people. Normal conversation is around 60 dB. Hearing guidelines use about 85 dB as a limit for long listening, and the recommended listening time drops quickly above that. Around 100 dB, it's only minutes, not hours. Many party speakers can go well past 100 dB up close. The real exposure depends on how far away you stand, the room and how long you listen, so a speaker's max-volume number isn't what your ears actually get. For most parties, you don't need anywhere near full volume.

### Battery Life & Charging
Battery life is listed as rated hours, measured under the maker's own test conditions, often at moderate volume. Expect fewer hours at high volume or with light shows on. Look for USB-C charging, and for power-bank output if you want to charge phones from the speaker.

### Protecting Your Hearing
Loud music over long stretches raises the risk of hearing damage. Kids often can't tell when it's too loud, and noise damage to hearing is permanent. Lower-risk habits: keep kids a few steps back from the speaker, keep the volume where people can talk without shouting, and take quiet breaks. These tips help keep listening within guidelines. They don't guarantee protection.

### FAQ
- *How loud should a party speaker be?* Loud enough to fill the space while people can still talk. Turning it up past that adds risk without making the party better.
- *Is a party speaker OK for a kids' party?* Yes, at lower volume and with kids kept back from the speaker. That's a lower-risk setup, not a guarantee.
- *How long will the battery last?* Use the rated hours as a best case. High volume and lights shorten it.
- *What does an IP rating mean?* It shows dust and water resistance. IPX4 handles splashes, and IP67 survives a brief dunk (about 1 m for 30 minutes in fresh water).
- *Can I pair two speakers?* Many models offer stereo or party pairing, but usually only with the same brand.
